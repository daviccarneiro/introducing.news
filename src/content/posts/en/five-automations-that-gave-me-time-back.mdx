I tested three automation platforms over six months. They all work; none survived the third month of real use. What remained was a small Worker with explicit rules and a readable log.

## What was left out

- Visual chains nobody reviews once they are live.
- Silent retries that hide integration failures.
- Flows that depend on a single service to stay alive.

## What replaced them

1. **One Worker per flow.** Each automation is a file with a name, an owner and an error budget.
2. **Structured logs.** If it failed, I know where, when and with which payload.
3. **A simple queue.** No need to process in real time what can wait five minutes.

The gain was not speed — it was predictability. An automation that breaks silently costs more than manual work.
