With every announcement of a new quantum processor, the press repeats the same script: broken cryptography, discovered drugs, solved logistics. It is worth separating what is already engineering from what is still a promise.

## Engineering vs. narrative

- **Error correction** left the whiteboard and became a scaling problem.
- **Physical qubits** grow in number; logical qubits, slowly.
- **Commercial applications** remain restricted to specific simulations.

> The interesting problem is not making one qubit work. It is making thousands of them work together, with an error rate low enough for the math to close.

## What to watch

Instead of following qubit announcements, follow publications on logical error rates. That is the number that actually tells how far we are from something useful.
